Professional profile

More than resolving MLS tickets.

My role requires understanding both the technical behavior of Multiple Listing Service (MLS) data and the operational impact of the decisions made around it. I investigate the immediate problem, determine where it originates, and consider how a change could affect other users, MLS integrations, workflows, or teams.

A missing listing may not be a listing problem. A broken filter may not be a filter problem. A mapping issue may expose a limitation in the product model. My responsibility is to determine which layer actually owns the problem and what solution makes sense beyond the immediate ticket.

In practice, my role sits between the real estate data supplied by MLS organizations and providers and the systems that turn that data into client-facing search, listings, and applications.

Flagship case study

Toronto Search Optimization

A recurring mapping problem became a product-level search architecture problem once every local fix started breaking another workflow.

I knew when a mapping problem was no longer a mapping problem.

TRREB largely represents Toronto through Area/County, while City can contain district-style values such as Toronto E07, W04, and C01. Lofty's City value was being reused across website search, Featured Areas, property alerts, and display - creating conflicting requirements.

Why it mattered
Search vs. alert conflict + documented client retention risk

A normalization that improved Toronto City search disrupted property alerts. Rolling it back restored alerts but brought the City-search problem back.

SourceTRREB structure
PlatformOne City value
ConflictSearch vs Alerts
DecisionStop patching
EscalationProduct / T3
OutcomeSearch optimized

What I did

  • Compared Toronto City and County search behavior
  • Reviewed TRREB Area, Municipality, and Community mappings
  • Tested City normalization and identified alert impact
  • Rolled the mapping back rather than leave a partial fix
  • Connected the behavior across multiple Jira cases
  • Escalated a stable optimization request to Product/T3

What the final solution needed to preserve

  • Intuitive Toronto-level search
  • TRREB district-level search behavior
  • Reliable property alerts
  • No broader address-model regression
  • A solution reusable across affected TRREB customers

How I work

The principles behind my decisions.

Investigate Before Changing

I validate what the MLS or provider actually supplies before modifying downstream behavior.

Question Assumptions

Existing system behavior is not automatically correct simply because it already exists.

Consider Wider Impact

A change that fixes one account but creates inconsistent behavior elsewhere is not automatically a good solution.

Know When to Stop Patching

If one mapping fix breaks another workflow, the product or data model may need to change.

Maintain Ownership

I stay accountable for driving cases forward even when the final solution depends on another team, MLS, or provider.

Look for Patterns

Multiple tickets can be symptoms of one provider, feed, migration, or platform issue.

Connect Technical & Operational Impact

A technically correct change still has to make sense for clients, workflows, and the teams supporting it.

Improve the Next Case

I look for ways each investigation can reduce repeat work or make future resolution more reliable.
